## Abstract

We describe the causal filling modulus procedure: a variational-topological construction that assigns a stiffness coefficient to every closed causal cycle in a causal information structure $(\mathcal{E}, \preceq, K, \rho)$. Ordinary topology classifies cycles as distinct or trivial but assigns no cost to them. The causal filling modulus repairs this omission by minimizing a Fisher-type information density $\mathcal{I}[\rho, K]$ over all surfaces $\Sigma$ bounded by a given cycle $\Gamma$, then normalizing the resulting infimum by the squared intrinsic causal diameter of the loop. The modulus is low when the causal information geometry is nearly flat and high when curvature is concentrated. At long wavelengths, the Euler–Lagrange equation of the filling functional produces the Einstein–Hilbert scalar $R$ as its unique two-derivative term, and variation gives Einstein's equation with a normalization coefficient $\alpha = 1/(16\pi G)$. The factor $16\pi = 4\pi \times 4$ is derived without empirical input: $4\pi$ is the angular volume of the celestial sphere of null directions at an event, and $4$ is the dimension of the causal bivector filling subspace $\mathcal{C}_p = \mathrm{span}\{\ell \wedge e_1, \ell \wedge e_2, n \wedge e_1, n \wedge e_2\}$ generated by the two null sheets and two transverse screen directions. The same procedure, applied to horizon boundaries, recovers the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy $S_\mathrm{BH} = k_B A_H / 4\ell_*^2$ with the factor $1/4$ arising as the inverse of the causal bivector degeneracy rather than as a thermodynamic postulate. Newton's constant is identified with the inverse gravitational stiffness, $G = 2\ell_0^2$, where $\ell_0$ is the primitive causal correlation length fixed by the Gaussian coarse-graining fixed point.

## 1. The Problem Topology Alone Cannot Solve

Standard topological invariants — homotopy groups, homology classes, Chern numbers — classify whether two cycles are the same or different. They do not answer the physically essential question: *how costly is it to fill a cycle?* In a causal information geometry, this cost has a direct interpretation. A causal cycle whose filling requires large information expenditure is a region of concentrated curvature, or strong gravitational field. A cycle that can be cheaply filled sits in nearly flat causal geometry.

The causal filling modulus is the object that bridges the topological and metrical questions. It is a positive real number assigned to each causal cycle by a minimax procedure over all allowable fillings, normalized to remove the trivial scale dependence of the loop diameter. Its properties determine the stiffness of the emergent gravitational theory.

The central claim of the filling procedure is the following:

> *Gravity is not a fundamental force imposed on a background spacetime. It is the long-wavelength stiffness theory of causal information transport, and Newton's constant is the inverse compliance of the minimal causal-curvature filling mode.*

## 2. Causal Information Structures

**Definition 2.1** (Causal information structure). A *causal information structure* is a quadruple

$$(\mathcal{E},\, \preceq,\, K,\, \rho),$$

where $\mathcal{E}$ is a set of elementary events, $\preceq$ is a causal preorder on $\mathcal{E}$, $K : \mathcal{E} \times \mathcal{E} \to \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ is a causal correlation kernel satisfying

$$K(x, y) = 0 \quad \text{unless } x \preceq y \text{ or } y \preceq x,$$

and $\rho : \mathcal{E} \to \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ is an event density.

The causal preorder is more primitive than metric time. At this level, $x \preceq y$ means only that information at $x$ can influence information at $y$. No background Lorentzian metric is assumed. The metric geometry of spacetime is an *output* of the filling procedure, not an input.

**Principle 2.2** (No metric smuggling). The kernel $K$ and order $\preceq$ must be defined without using a background Lorentzian metric. A Lorentzian metric may appear only after taking the continuum, Hessian, or spectral limit of the causal information structure.

## 3. Causal Cycles and Their Filling Cost

**Definition 3.1** (Causal cycle). A *causal cycle* $\Gamma$ is a closed chain in the coarse-grained order complex of events,

$$x_0 \preceq x_1 \preceq \cdots \preceq x_n, \qquad x_n \sim x_0,$$

where $\sim$ denotes coarse-grained informational equivalence — not literal closed timelike motion, which is prohibited by the causal preorder.

**Definition 3.2** (Causal filling cost). Given a causal cycle $\Gamma$, its *filling cost* is

$$S_\mathrm{fill}(\Gamma) = \inf_{\Sigma\,:\,\partial\Sigma = \Gamma} \int_\Sigma \mathcal{I}[\rho, K],$$

where $\mathcal{I}[\rho, K]$ is a Fisher-type information density constructed from causal correlations. The infimum runs over all surfaces $\Sigma$ bounded by $\Gamma$ in the causal order complex.

The information density $\mathcal{I}[\rho, K]$ is the Fisher information of the causal transition kernel, computed locally at each event:

$$\mathcal{I}[\rho, K](x) = \int K(x, y) \left(\delta \log \rho(x) - \delta \log \rho(y)\right)^2 d\mu(y).$$

This is positive, vanishes when the density is locally constant (flat causal geometry), and diverges at curvature singularities. It is constructed from the causal kernel $K$ alone and carries no background metric dependence.

**Definition 3.3** (Causal filling modulus). The *causal filling modulus* of a cycle $\Gamma$ is

$$\boxed{\kappa_\mathrm{caus}(\Gamma) = \frac{S_\mathrm{fill}(\Gamma)}{\tau_\Gamma^2},}$$

where $\tau_\Gamma$ is the intrinsic causal diameter of the loop — the maximum correlation depth between any two events on $\Gamma$, measured by the kernel $K$ rather than by a prior metric.

Three properties follow immediately. First, $\kappa_\mathrm{caus}(\Gamma) \geq 0$, since both numerator and denominator are positive. Second, $\kappa_\mathrm{caus}(\Gamma) = 0$ if and only if the cycle can be filled at zero information cost, which occurs precisely when the causal information geometry is flat inside $\Gamma$. Third, the normalization by $\tau_\Gamma^2$ makes the modulus dimensionless in causal units and independent of the overall scale of the loop, so it captures the *shape* of the curvature rather than the accidental size of the region.

## 5. The Filling Functional and Its Long-Wavelength Expansion

The full causal filling action on a region is

$$S_\mathrm{fill}[g] = \inf_{\Sigma : \partial\Sigma = \Gamma} \int_\Sigma \mathcal{I}[\rho, K].$$

In the continuum, coarse-grained limit, locality and diffeomorphism invariance strongly constrain the form of this functional. A systematic derivative expansion in curvature invariants gives

$$S_\mathrm{fill}[g] = \int d^4x \sqrt{-g} \left[\Lambda_\mathrm{eff} + \alpha R + c_2 \ell_*^2 R^2 + c_3 \ell_*^2 R_{\mu\nu} R^{\mu\nu} + \cdots \right].$$

The zeroth-order term $\Lambda_\mathrm{eff}$ is the cosmological constant, identified below as the IR causal-boundary zero mode. The leading nontrivial two-derivative term is $\alpha R$, where $R$ is the Ricci scalar. This is not an assumption: $R$ is the *unique* local, diffeomorphism-invariant, two-derivative scalar in four dimensions. Any filling functional satisfying the locality and diffeomorphism conditions of the causal information structure must have $R$ as its leading curvature term.

**Proposition 5.1** (Einstein–Hilbert as leading filling elasticity). If the causal filling functional is local, diffeomorphism-invariant in the continuum limit, and second-order in derivatives at leading order, then its leading curvature term is proportional to $\int \sqrt{-g}\, R$.

Varying the total action $S_\mathrm{total} = S_\mathrm{fill}[g] + S_m[g, \psi]$ with respect to $g^{\mu\nu}$ and using $\delta\int\sqrt{-g}\,R = \int\sqrt{-g}(R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2}R g_{\mu\nu})\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ gives

$$\alpha G_{\mu\nu} + \frac{1}{2}\Lambda_\mathrm{eff} g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{2} T_{\mu\nu},$$

which is Einstein's equation once $\alpha = 1/(16\pi G)$.

The task of the filling modulus programme is therefore to derive this coefficient from the causal structure alone, without empirical input.

## 6. Derivation of the $16\pi$ Coefficient

The coefficient $\alpha = 1/(16\pi G)$ has two independent factors: the $16\pi$ angular normalization and the gravitational stiffness $G^{-1}$. The filling procedure derives both.

### 6.1 Celestial-Sphere Normalization: the $4\pi$ Factor

At any event $p$ in the continuum limit, the space of future null directions modulo overall scale is the celestial sphere

$$\mathcal{N}_p \simeq S^2.$$

An isotropic causal filling must average over all null directions at $p$. The angular volume of this average is

$$\mathrm{Vol}(S^2) = 4\pi.$$

This factor is fixed by the four-dimensional nature of spacetime and by the isotropy of the filling prescription. It does not depend on the specific causal kernel.

### 6.2 Causal Bivector Trace: the Factor of 4

At an event $p$, choose a null tetrad:

$$\ell^\mu, \quad n^\mu, \quad e_1^\mu, \quad e_2^\mu,$$

with $\ell^2 = n^2 = 0$, $\ell \cdot n = -1$, $e_A \cdot e_B = \delta_{AB}$, and all cross products vanishing. The future null sheet of a causal diamond has area bivectors $B_A^+ = \ell \wedge e_A$ for $A = 1, 2$; the past null sheet has $B_A^- = n \wedge e_A$.

**Definition 6.1** (Causal filling bivector subspace). The *causal filling bivector subspace* at $p$ is

$$\mathcal{C}_p = \mathrm{span}\{\ell \wedge e_1,\; \ell \wedge e_2,\; n \wedge e_1,\; n \wedge e_2\}.$$

**Theorem 6.2** (Causal bivector dimension). $\dim \mathcal{C}_p = 4$ and $\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{C}_p}(I) = 4$.

The full bivector space $\Lambda^2 T_p M$ decomposes as

$$\Lambda^2 T_p M = \mathrm{span}\{\ell \wedge n\} \oplus \mathrm{span}\{e_1 \wedge e_2\} \oplus \mathcal{C}_p.$$

The components $\ell \wedge n$ and $e_1 \wedge e_2$ are the radial boost and screen rotation modes respectively. They do not generate causal area transport along the null sheets, so the filling trace runs over $\mathcal{C}_p$ rather than the full six-dimensional bivector space.

The physical content is clear: a causal diamond has two null boundary sheets (future and past), each spanning two transverse screen directions. The four modes of $\mathcal{C}_p$ are the four independent ways to transport causal area information across this diamond. Any isotropic normalization of the filling action must trace over exactly these four modes.

### 6.3 The Combined Result

**Theorem 6.3** (Causal curvature normalization). For an isotropic four-dimensional causal information structure whose curvature degrees of freedom are rank-2 area-transport modes, the normalized minimal causal-curvature angular volume is

$$\Omega_\mathrm{grav} = \mathrm{Vol}(S^2) \cdot \mathrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{C}_p}(I) = 4\pi \cdot 4 = 16\pi.$$

The leading two-derivative filling action is therefore

$$\boxed{S_\mathrm{fill}^{(2)} = \frac{\kappa_\mathrm{grav}}{16\pi} \int d^4x \sqrt{-g}\, R,}$$

and with $\kappa_\mathrm{grav} = G^{-1}$ this is the Einstein–Hilbert action with the correct normalization.

## 7. Newton's Constant as Inverse Filling Stiffness

The stiffness $\kappa_\mathrm{grav} = G^{-1}$ is itself determined by the causal kernel. The emergent metric is the Hessian of the causal information action,

$$g_{\mu\nu}^\mathrm{eff}(x) = \left.\frac{\delta^2 S_\mathrm{info}}{\delta\xi^\mu(x)\,\delta\xi^\nu(x)}\right|_{\xi=0} = \rho(x)\, M^{\mu\nu}(x),$$

where $M^{\mu\nu}(x) = \int d^4\xi\, \mathcal{K}_x(\xi)\, \xi^\mu \xi^\nu$ is the second-moment tensor of the causal correlation kernel. The stiffness formula then follows:

$$\kappa_\mathrm{grav} = \frac{C_F}{\ell_0^2}, \qquad G = \frac{\ell_0^2}{C_F}.$$

The Gaussian coarse-graining fixed point theorem establishes $C_F = 1/2$: under repeated independent causal composition, the unique stable long-wavelength kernel is Gaussian, and a Gaussian kernel written as $\exp(-\xi^2/\ell_0^2)$ has second-moment coefficient $\ell_0^2/2$ per tangent direction. Therefore

$$\boxed{G = 2\ell_0^2}$$

in natural units. The factor of 2 is not a fit; it is the inverse of the Gaussian second moment — a consequence of the coarse-graining fixed point theorem applied to the causal kernel.

The primitive causal correlation length $\ell_0 = \ell_P/\sqrt{2}$ is related to the Planck length $\ell_P = \sqrt{\hbar G/c^3}$ by a $\sqrt{2}$ factor reflecting the distinction between the raw microscopic causal spacing and the effective curvature scale.

## 8. Horizon Consequences of the Filling Procedure

The filling modulus procedure has two immediate consequences for horizon physics that provide independent checks of the normalization.

### 8.1 Bekenstein–Hawking Entropy as Boundary Filling Count

A black-hole horizon is a causal filling singularity: causal transport stiffness diverges, minimal fillings become non-extendable, and information escape cost localizes at the horizon. The entropy counts admissible microscopic boundary fillings.

For horizon area $A_H$ and effective filling area $\ell_*^2$, the number of elementary boundary modes is $N_H = A_H/\ell_*^2$. Because the physical gravitational boundary mode is a causal bivector mode with $\dim \mathcal{C}_p = 4$, one physical horizon bit occupies four elementary causal area cells:

$$N_\mathrm{phys} = \frac{A_H}{4\ell_*^2}.$$

The dimensionless entropy is therefore

$$\boxed{S_\mathrm{BH} = k_B N_\mathrm{phys} = \frac{k_B A_H}{4\ell_*^2} = \frac{k_B c^3 A_H}{4\hbar G}.}$$

The factor $1/4$ is not inserted thermodynamically. It is the inverse of the causal bivector degeneracy $\dim \mathcal{C}_p = 4$, the same factor that appears in the normalization of the filling action. This is the Bekenstein–Hawking area law recovered as a boundary filling count.

### 8.2 Cosmological Constant as the IR Zero Mode

The zeroth-order term in the derivative expansion of $S_\mathrm{fill}$ is the zero-mode curvature of the largest causal filling boundary. For a de Sitter-like causal horizon of radius $R_C$,

$$\boxed{\Lambda = \frac{3}{R_C^2}.}$$

In flat $\Lambda$CDM, $R_C = c/(H_0\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda})$, giving $\Lambda = 3H_0^2\Omega_\Lambda/c^2$. With Planck 2018 parameters ($H_0 = 67.4$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$, $\Omega_\Lambda \simeq 0.685$), this evaluates to $\Lambda = 1.0909 \times 10^{-52}$ m$^{-2}$, within 0.27% of the PDG value $1.088(30) \times 10^{-52}$ m$^{-2}$.

The cosmological constant is thereby reframed from a UV vacuum-energy density — where it is famously problematic — to an IR causal-boundary quantity. Its small observed value is not a fine-tuning problem in this picture; it is the natural result of filling at the largest available causal scale.

## 10. Open Obligations

The filling procedure as described leaves several obligations open.

The most important is the derivation of the absolute scale $\ell_0$. The pure gravitational sector establishes the form $G = 2\ell_0^2$ but cannot fix $\ell_0$ without a scale-setting mechanism. In the companion *Physical Sector* paper, cross-sector stiffness matching fixes $\ell_0 = (\sqrt{2}M_*)^{-1}$ where $M_*$ is the UV vacuum stiffness scale probed by the charged spinorial electron sector. This closes the chain conditionally: the $0.022\%$ agreement between the predicted and observed value of $Gm_e^2 = 4\exp(-64\pi^3/19)$ is the empirical content of this matching.

The causal Lichnerowicz spectrum — the eigenvalues of the causal filling operator $\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{grav}$ acting on curvature perturbation modes — remains to be computed explicitly. The spectral Newton coupling relation $\kappa_\mathrm{grav} = \lambda_\mathrm{grav,min}/S_\mathrm{curv}$ then gives $G$ purely from the discrete causal event process.

The derivation of the cosmic causal radius $R_C$ from the microscopic event process, rather than from the cosmological parameters $H_0$ and $\Omega_\Lambda$, would make $\Lambda$ genuinely parameter-free. This is the deepest open problem in the IR sector.

Finally, the higher-curvature coefficients $c_2, c_3, \ldots$ in the derivative expansion should be derivable from the specific form of the causal correlation kernel at subleading order in the coarse-graining. Their computation would provide falsifiable predictions for quantum-gravitational corrections to classical GR in high-curvature regimes.

## 11. Conclusion

The causal filling modulus procedure assigns gravitational stiffness to causal cycles by a minimax variational principle over causal information costs, normalized by the squared causal diameter of the loop. Its long-wavelength expansion produces the Einstein–Hilbert action as its unique two-derivative term. The normalization coefficient $16\pi = 4\pi \times 4$ is derived from the geometry of null directions: $4\pi$ from the celestial-sphere angular volume of future null rays at an event, and $4$ from the dimension of the causal bivector filling subspace $\mathcal{C}_p$ generated by the two null sheets and two transverse screen directions of a causal diamond. Newton's constant is the inverse gravitational stiffness, $G = 2\ell_0^2$, where the factor of $2$ is the inverse Gaussian second-moment coefficient forced by the coarse-graining fixed point theorem.

The same causal bivector degeneracy that produces the $1/16\pi G$ coefficient in the bulk action produces the $1/4$ in the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy formula — both as traces over $\mathcal{C}_p$, not as independent thermodynamic postulates. The cosmological constant emerges as the IR zero-mode curvature of the largest causal filling boundary, reframing the cosmological constant problem from a UV fine-tuning question to an IR causal-boundary question.

The filling modulus is the gravitational member of a family of analogous objects across the programme. The spinorial defect escape modulus fixes the gauge coupling and electron mass. The causal filling modulus fixes Newton's constant and the Einstein normalization. The Arithmetic Vacuum's Möbius operator is the discrete arithmetic analogue of both. All three assign a stiffness or cost to a topological structure by a variational procedure, and all three produce coupling constants as inverse stiffnesses without free parameters.
